### [Lovable-built apps leaked passport data and 200K+ messages via weak row-level security](https://www.crissyfield.de/research/lovable-en/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Security researchers at Crissy Field found that applications built with the AI app-generation platform Lovable exposed sensitive user data, including passport details and more than 200,000 private messages, because the generated apps had insufficient Row-Level Security on their databases. Reportedly, the data could be accessed by merely inspecting network calls in the browser, meaning no sophisticated attack was required. Lovable&\#x27;s head of security responded in the discussion that the company automatically scans every app before publish, offers a deeper SAST scan free to all users, and lets users apply one-click fixes, enable background auto-fix, and block publishing. The disclosure has renewed debate over whether insecure defaults in AI coding platforms and their backend providers leave non-expert builders shipping exposed databases.

hackernews · armcat · Nov 14, 17:43 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50499017)

**「Row-Level Security and Supabase defaults」** Row-Level Security \(RLS\) is the Postgres feature that enforces authorization rules inside the database, and Supabase — the hosted backend service behind Lovable-generated apps — warns in its own documentation that a table in an exposed schema without RLS is readable and writable by any role with a grant on it. Supabase has nonetheless left RLS disabled by default on new tables, and users have been asking the company to enable it by default since at least March 2024, arguing that the permissive setting invites accidental public data leaks. Apps generated by Lovable therefore started with open database tables unless RLS policies were explicitly added, making the platform&\#x27;s and Supabase&\#x27;s defaults the decisive factor in whether data stayed private.

**「Impact」** Developers and organizations running apps built on Lovable face a concrete exposure risk: Crissy Field&\#x27;s scan of 159,041 Lovable projects found passport numbers, payment details, and more than 200,000 chat messages in databases reachable straight from a browser with no login, so any Lovable-built app with similarly weak Row-Level Security is likely readable to strangers using nothing more than browser network inspection. Lovable&\#x27;s Head of Security says the platform automatically scans every app before publish and offers a free deeper SAST scan with one-click and background auto-fix options, so operators of Lovable-built apps should run these scans and manually verify their Row-Level Security settings rather than assume defaults are safe. Commenters on Hacker News additionally warn that Supabase&\#x27;s October 30 change to its permissive defaults reportedly does not apply to existing projects, meaning older backends need manual review instead of relying on the platform-side fix.

**「Debate over blame: Lovable or Supabase defaults」** Commenters disagreed over root cause: one argued Supabase bears significant responsibility for permissive defaults chosen years ago to ease onboarding, claiming a fix landed on October 30 but that existing projects may still run with the old defaults, while Lovable&\#x27;s head of security maintained its pre-publish scanning exists precisely to catch insufficient Row-Level Security. Others argued the broader lesson is that platforms need deny-by-default databases and least-privilege APIs, since AI-generated apps are numerous, exploitable without sophisticated skills, and often built by people who cannot evaluate the risk.

The affected parties would be GPU buyers across the AI industry, including funded startups, cloud providers, and other customers paying above what an unconstrained market might charge. The claims remain unverified: this is secondary reporting on allegations, with no regulatory finding or confirmation from Nvidia, and similar circular-financing concerns have circulated in prior coverage rather than being newly documented.

hackernews · madihaa · Nov 14, 22:26 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50501631)

**「Background」** Circular financing refers to a supplier funding its own customers, whose spending then flows back as revenue — in this case, Nvidia investing in AI startups that buy its GPUs. The concern predates this report: the Bank for International Settlements&\#x27; annual economic report, published June 28, 2026, flagged chip makers, hyperscalers, and AI labs being tied through opaque private arrangements, including circular financing where equity stakes sit beside multi-year chip or compute commitments, and a July 27, 2026 report described Nvidia&\#x27;s roughly $750 billion AI financing web as drawing scrutiny over circular money flows. Coverage published alongside the TechSpot piece attributes the underlying allegation to a Wall Street Journal investigation claiming Nvidia systematically funded AI startups on condition that they use the proceeds to buy its GPUs.

**「Impact on GPU buyers」** Buyers of Nvidia&\#x27;s high-end cards face the most direct consequence: one commenter reports the 96GB RTX 6000 Blackwell currently sells for roughly $16,000 against an $8,000 MSRP, and argues that a fall toward MSRP would make running open models like Qwen locally cheaper than paying AI subscriptions — a concrete decision point for households and small companies weighing on-prem inference against cloud plans. The reported mechanism works against that outcome for now: Nvidia&\#x27;s funding of AI startups&\#x27; datacenter expansion absorbed surplus silicon &\#x27;instead of letting the supply flood the market and force GPU prices down,&\#x27; and one report puts its 2026 ecosystem deployments above $40 billion, including a $30 billion OpenAI stake — leaving organizations planning large GPU purchases or multi-year cloud commitments unable to tell whether current scarcity pricing reflects durable demand. Another commenter counters that high GPU rental rates and cloud providers&\#x27; own statements indicate the compute shortage is genuine, in which case prices would stay elevated even if the financing arrangements stopped.

**「Community debate」** Commenters split on intent: k0morebi argued the startup investments were ordinary demand stimulation rather than anything nefarious, while wqnt countered that high GPU rental prices and statements from major cloud and inference providers indicate genuinely real compute shortage rather than manufactured demand. hadlock argued the practical stakes are local AI adoption, citing the 96GB Blackwell RTX 6000 selling near $16,000 against an $8,000 MSRP and claiming that cheaper high-end cards would let households and small companies run models locally instead of paying subscriptions.

### [PyPI project pages now surface provenance and security signals](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2026-11-06-ui-updates-complete/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

PyPI has completed a redesign of its project pages that makes provenance and other security signals more prominent, according to an official PyPI blog post dated November 6, 2026. The change does not introduce new security capabilities: provenance, an attestation produced through OIDC-based trusted publishing, and trusted publishing itself already existed, and the redesign brings these signals onto the page where users evaluate and download packages. The announcement prompted substantive Hacker News discussion about what the now-visible signals can and cannot establish about a package&\#x27;s trustworthiness.

hackernews · rbanffy · Nov 14, 15:40 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50497892)

**「Background」** Provenance and per-file attestations are supply-chain security signals on PyPI, the Python Package Index, that let users verify a release was published through OIDC-based trusted publishing from the maintainer&\#x27;s CI/CD pipeline rather than uploaded manually; these capabilities predate the redesign, which changes how prominently they are displayed rather than introducing them. The work was set up in a July 22, 2026 PyPI announcement of planned interface updates — shaped by user research with community members — to improve how security signals and package details are surfaced, to be staged on TestPyPI and rolled out to production in phases.

**「Practical impact」** Package consumers can now see, on each project&\#x27;s files section, whether a release carries provenance, making it easier to distinguish releases published through trusted publishing from those that were not. Maintainers who want the signal must publish via trusted publishing, which depends on OIDC support in their CI/CD setup; projects published without it will stand out as lacking the attestation.

**「Community debate」** Commenters debated what the signals actually guarantee: cowlevel argued that provenance detects projects whose PyPI publishing was compromised, not projects that are inherently malicious, while time4tea objected that anchoring trust in OIDC-based trusted publishing centralizes it with &quot;big cloud&quot; providers and risks lock-in. djoldman illustrated the signal&\#x27;s uneven adoption with live examples — datasette&\#x27;s files section shows provenance while dlt&\#x27;s does not — and mhils asked that trusted publishing add support for GitHub reusable workflows \(warehouse issue \#11096\).

### [Project Zero to cut vulnerability disclosure window from 90 to 30 days by 2028](https://projectzero.google/2026/11/disclosure-policy.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Google Project Zero has announced a phased shortening of its vulnerability disclosure deadline: as of the announcement, bugs are published 90 days after being reported to the vendor \(a stage the team calls &quot;Full 90&quot;\), dropping to 60 days on February 2, 2027, and 30 days on January 3, 2028. Project Zero, Google&\#x27;s in-house bug-hunting team, has published unpatched flaws on a 90-day clock since its 2014 founding, so the 60- and 30-day windows are announced future changes rather than rules in effect today. The schedule applies to any vendor, open-source project, or enterprise that receives a Project Zero report, cutting the time available to develop, test, and ship a fix to as little as one-third of the current window.

hackernews · hacb · Nov 14, 11:13 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50496051)

**「Background」** Google Project Zero is the company&\#x27;s in-house security research team that reports vulnerabilities to affected vendors and publishes technical details publicly once a fixed deadline passes, an approach intended to pressure vendors into shipping patches rather than leaving flaws known only to researchers unaddressed. Until this announcement, the team&\#x27;s baseline deadline was 90 days after a bug is reported — the &quot;Full 90&quot; window the newly announced schedule steps down from. In announcing the change, the team argued that AI tools are speeding up attackers, so keeping a reported bug secret protects users less than it once did, though it still sees some short-term benefit in delay.

**「Slow-patch vendors and long-cycle distributions face the squeeze」** Vendors and downstream distributors with slow release cycles face the tightest squeeze: under the &quot;Full 30&quot; policy taking effect January 3, 2028, they will have only 30 days from Project Zero&\#x27;s report to develop, test, and ship a fix before technical details are published. Distributions that repackage and validate those fixes inherit whatever time remains — Ubuntu, for example, prepares security updates within defined per-release maintenance windows and publishes them as security notices, and one commenter argued it &quot;will need a LOT more time than that.&quot; Organizations running long-term-support releases should track Project Zero disclosures alongside their distribution&\#x27;s security notices and prepare for faster emergency patching as the 60-day \(February 2, 2027\) and 30-day deadlines phase in.

**「Community reaction」** Commenters questioned whether the compressed deadlines fit slower movers: one argued that Ubuntu, with its long support cycles, &quot;will need a LOT more time than that,&quot; while another contended Project Zero should &quot;release right away,&quot; since nothing short of immediate publication compels a vendor to fix. Both points are opinions about the policy&\#x27;s likely effects rather than established outcomes.

### [EFF argues tech companies are redefining privacy norms amid AI recording tools](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/11/tech-companies-are-trying-shift-entire-concept-privacy-again) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On November 14, 2026, the Electronic Frontier Foundation published an analysis arguing that tech companies are attempting to shift prevailing privacy norms as AI-driven recording tools — such as workplace meeting notetakers and transcription devices — become routine in everyday communication. The piece is an advocacy argument about industry direction rather than a new capability, standard, or rule, and the available material does not name specific companies, products, or proposed rules. It drew heavy engagement on Hacker News \(478 points, 176 comments\), where the debate centered on pervasive meeting transcription, the legal and human-rights basis of privacy, and whether transcription devices&\#x27; accessibility benefits can be separated from data-retention risks.

hackernews · gslin · Nov 14, 12:45 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50496602)

**「AI recorders vs. consent-based privacy」** Privacy expectations have long assumed that recording is visible or announced, so the people being captured can object — an assumption now strained by AI notetaker bots that join workplace meetings and by recording-capable smart glasses, which can capture conversations without everyone present being aware. In a November 13, 2026 report, EFF argues that companies including Meta, Google, Apple, and Amazon are reframing the issue so that individuals must become experts in nearby devices&\#x27; capabilities and whether they signal recording, rather than those doing the recording bearing the burden of consent.

**「Impact」** Workers are the most immediately affected group: according to one commenter&\#x27;s account, AI notetakers now record and upload essentially every professional meeting, including one-on-ones between managers and direct reports, and opting out seems incompatible with staying employed. The concrete countermeasure raised in the discussion is meeting-level consent — asking all participants to turn off AI notetakers before proceeding — though the commenter noted no established norm or guarantee currently backs such a request.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters diverged on remedies: one invoked Article 12 of the UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights to argue that companies have no right to arbitrary interference with private correspondence, while another called individual fixes insufficient and recommended personal mitigations — a real-name public profile, throwaway identities, air gaps, and funding digital-rights groups such as EFF, IJ, and EPIC. A separate argument pushed back on restricting transcription hardware, contending that live-transcription glasses could be transformative for deaf and hard-of-hearing users even with local-only processing, but that retention remains &quot;one if-statement away&quot; and bans would also catch open-source assistive tools.

### [Cloudflare Open-Sources Kitesurf, a Stateless Browser for AI Agents](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Cloudflare-Kitesurf-Open-Source) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Cloudflare has released Kitesurf as open source under the Apache 2.0 license, with the code published on GitHub. Kitesurf is a stateless browser aimed at AI agents, built on Cloudflare Workers, that parses HTML, handles CSS, and executes JavaScript without running a full browser container. Cloudflare positions it as a lightweight alternative to headless Chromium, claiming lower CPU and memory usage and the ability to scale on demand across the company&\#x27;s global Workers network. The resource advantages are vendor claims; the source report, based on Phoronix&\#x27;s coverage, includes no benchmarks or independent measurements.

telegram · zaihuapd · Nov 14, 09:33

**「Background」** AI agents that browse the web have typically relied on headless Chromium, a full browser engine that is resource-intensive to run, and that is the status quo Kitesurf is built to replace. The project is an experimental browser engine assembled from several open-source Rust projects and runs entirely on Cloudflare Workers, the company&\#x27;s serverless platform for executing code across its global edge network. The open-source release follows through on a plan Cloudflare had described earlier, when it said it intended to publish Kitesurf &quot;once we&\#x27;re ready&quot; so customers could deploy their own copies on their own accounts.

**「Impact for agent developers」** Teams running browser-based AI agent workloads gain a permissively licensed \(Apache 2.0\) alternative to self-managed headless Chromium: third-party writeups report Kitesurf uses roughly 3.1x–3.8x less CPU and 4.7x–7x less memory than headless Chromium, and it can be driven through Puppeteer, MCP, or curl. Because Kitesurf is a Rust rewrite rather than a Chromium wrapper, developers should verify Chrome DevTools Protocol compatibility and site rendering fidelity before migrating, since some agent workflows may still require full Chromium.

The offering, led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, is set to price on November 18 and begin trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT the following day.

**「Impact」** Mainland Chinese and Hong Kong institutional investors are barred from placing direct orders in the IPO under US arms-export rules, and some are reportedly seeking exposure instead through British Virgin Islands trusts, special-purpose vehicles, and swap structures.
