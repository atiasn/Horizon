### [Thinkingbox-Bench grades AI agents on 20-attempt reliability via database state](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1x17shf/thinkingbox_solving_an_agent_task_once_vs_solving/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Researchers affiliated with Microsoft released Thinkingbox-Bench, a public benchmark that grades agent reliability across 507 policy-conditioned business workflows in 5 domains \(retail, travel/hospitality, auto insurance, neobank internal IT, and consulting IT/HR\). Each task is run in 20 independently executed attempts from an identical clean backend state—10,140 trials per model—with grading based on the terminal database state and side effects rather than surface task completion; 477 of 507 tasks are graded on state alone. The benchmark distinguishes three metrics that rank models very differently: Kimi-K3 solved 93.89% of tasks at least once but only 13.41% on all 20 attempts, while a second model discovered fewer tasks \(79.09%\) but repeated far more reliably \(47.53%\). The authors also found that 67.24% of the 79,853 executable-check failures across 12 models terminated cleanly without tool errors, meaning completion-style evaluation proxies would have scored them as done. The paper, code, and dataset are public, and the benchmark is available on Hugging Face OpenEnv, though raw evaluation trajectories are not released.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/tuhin\_k · Oct 9, 00:50

**「Background」** Agent benchmarks have typically measured success as a single attempt completing a task, often judged by the agent&\#x27;s final answer or trajectory rather than by verifying what state a backend system actually ended up in. Thinkingbox-Bench departs from this by grading 507 policy-conditioned workflows across domains like retail, hospitality, auto insurance, neobank IT, and consulting IT/HR on terminal database state, and by separating one-off discovery \(pass@1, pass@20\) from repeated reliability \(all-20 successes\) — metrics the authors argue are routinely conflated in agent leaderboards.

**「Why it matters」** Teams selecting agents for business workflows should note that ranking by pass@20 \(solved at least once\) versus all-20 \(solved every time\) produces nearly reversed leaderboards in this benchmark—for example, Qwen3.8-27B reached 89.35% at-least-once success but only 7.50% every-time success—so single-attempt or best-of-N numbers may overstate reliability for stateful enterprise work. The authors note the tasks are synthetic reconstructions of enterprise workflow patterns rather than production traffic, the all-20 figure is an observed count on a fixed trial budget rather than a guarantee of future reliability, and the simulated user is a fixed LLM that introduces variance. Organizations can run all 507 tasks against their own models through the Hugging Face OpenEnv environment to generate their own numbers.

### [Anthropic Launches Opt-In AI Vulnerability Scanner for Open Source Projects](https://www.anthropic.com/research/launching-opt-in-vuln-finding-service-for-open-source) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Anthropic has launched OSS Scanner, a free, opt-in vulnerability scanning service for qualifying open-source projects, announced via its research blog. Reports are generated by Claude and other models without human review, and include a vulnerability reproduction, an explanation, and, where possible, patch suggestions — with Anthropic noting the reports may contain errors. The company says the service found over 29,000 candidate vulnerabilities in the past six months, of which roughly 6,000 were manually reviewed; in early testing, 85 of 97 high-severity or critical findings met the requirements of its disclosure process. Core maintainers of eligible projects can apply by submitting a GitHub PR.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 9, 02:00

**「Background」** OSS Scanner builds on Anthropic&\#x27;s earlier Project Glasswing, an effort that used Claude to discover vulnerabilities in open-source software; the company says the new service&\#x27;s approach is informed by that experience. The opt-in model means eligible projects must actively apply — core maintainers submit a GitHub PR to enroll — rather than being scanned automatically.

**「Maintainers can apply, but should treat reports as unverified」** Open-source maintainers of eligible projects can request the service by submitting a GitHub PR, but because the AI-generated reports are not human-reviewed, findings may include false positives or flawed patch suggestions and should be independently validated before acting on them.
